Bounds for dynamic identifying codes in the king grid

Yasuhito Tsujimura

Universion Closure Logic · ORCID ↗

Public version: 2026-11-05Version 1.0.0English

Abstract

A dynamic identifying code is a walk whose visited vertices form an identifying code; its cost counts every visit, including repetitions. Let M_n be the minimum cost in the n × n king grid. We prove that, for every n ≥ 6, 28n²/113 − 7n ≤ M_n ≤ n²/4 + 36n, and that the limit d_dyn = lim(n→∞) M_n/n² exists. Consequently, 28/113 ≤ d_dyn ≤ 1/4.

The lower bound combines a fixed integer discharging certificate with the inequality M ≥ K + L − 2, where a walk visits K distinct vertices and their induced graph has L leaves. The upper bound is given by explicit walks through a periodic diagonal pattern, with boundary overhead linear in n. A separate gluing argument proves existence of the limit. Two further certificates give density lower bounds 6/25 and 12/49 for total dominating and connected identifying codes, respectively, in the infinite king grid. The certificates can be checked by exhaustive integer computation without re-solving an optimization problem. The exact dynamic density remains open; the bounds leave an interval of width 1/452.

What this result provides

Finite-size bounds and existence of the limiting dynamic identifying-code cost in the king grid.

Scope

The cost counts every visit, including repetitions. The limiting value lies in [28/113, 1/4]; its exact value remains open.
