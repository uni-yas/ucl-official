PERSPECTIVE

Our research perspective

Complex problems in mathematics and theoretical physics can contain aspects that do not require detailed examination, while even a slight oversight in others can substantially alter the conclusion. UCL uses mathematical approaches to investigate these differences.

We explore when a correct answer can be reached without examining every element. When the desired conclusion still cannot be drawn, we carefully investigate which clues are missing and the fundamental reasons why.

Quantum marginals and sharp error guarantees

When local observations are close to a target, how close must the full quantum state be?

Read paper ↗
Summary & scopeClose details＋

Zenodo · Preprint
Published 11 November 2026

Submission to arXiv is planned.

Overview

For specified quantum states, we prove sharp limits on the global error guarantees obtainable from local observations. With the same twelve-qubit target and observation map, the strength of the guarantee depends on whether competing states may be entangled.

Scope of the result

For a fixed twelve-qubit product target and all 66 two-qubit marginals, the sharp trace-norm error exponent is 1/2 for competitors separable across the six:six cut and 1/4 for unrestricted competitors. Adding the three-qubit marginals of one six-qubit block restores exponent 1/2 for unrestricted competitors. Sharpness concerns the worst-case asymptotic exponent as the observation error tends to zero.

Exactly which hexagonal strip widths attain density 1/3?

How does strip width change the density needed for locating-domination?

Read paper ↗
Summary & scopeClose details＋

Zenodo · Preprint
Published 11 November 2026

Overview

For an infinite hexagonal strip, the minimum locating-dominating density is 1/3 exactly when the width is divisible by 3. At the other widths, the strip boundary forces a positive density excess.

Scope of the result

For width k not divisible by 3, the excess is of order 1/k². Exact densities are proved for widths 7, 8 and 10, with lower bounds covering nonperiodic configurations. The general exact-density conjecture remains open.

Tuza's conjecture and triangle overlap

How can triangle-overlap conditions extend the class of graphs satisfying Tuza's inequality?

Read paper ↗
Summary & scopeClose details＋

arXiv:2611.11128v1 · Preprint

Overview

We prove Tuza's inequality for finite simple graphs under specified triangle-overlap and density conditions. We also localize the density condition to the subgraph induced by the vertices of the Hifumi core, as defined in the paper.

Scope of the result

The inequality holds when each edge belongs to at most four triangles, the edges belonging to four triangles form components with at most two edges, and the maximum average degree is at most 25/2. The paper gives a stronger strict bound and its conditions. This is not a proof of the general Tuza conjecture.

Width-eleven strip

What sensor density is sufficient for a hexagonal strip eleven rows wide?

Read paper ↗
Summary & scopeClose details＋

Overview

Sensors must distinguish every unselected vertex in an infinite strip eleven rows wide. This paper determines the exact minimum locating-dominating density: 48/143.

Details

The lower bound 48/143 holds for all locating-dominating sets, including nonperiodic ones. A 26-column periodic construction selects 96 of 286 vertices and attains equality. This settles width eleven in Gomes and Wakabayashi's Conjecture 9, not the conjecture at all widths.

Walks on the king grid

How many visits are needed to identify locations while moving, when revisits also count?

Read paper ↗
Summary & scopeClose details＋

Overview

A walk on the king grid may move horizontally, vertically, or diagonally. Its visited vertices must form an identifying code. This paper bounds the number of visits, counting repeated visits to the same vertex.

Details

For an n×n board with n≥6, the minimum number of visits M_n satisfies 28n²/113 − 7n ≤ M_n ≤ n²/4 + 36n. The limit of M_n/n² exists and lies between 28/113 and 1/4. Whether the limit equals 1/4 remains open.

Can sensors distinguish every location in the kagome grid without sensing their own vertices?

Read paper ↗
Summary & scopeClose details＋

Overview

In the kagome grid of triangles and hexagons, every vertex, including sensor locations, must have a distinct nonempty set of neighboring sensors. This paper establishes the exact minimum open-locating-dominating density of 2/5.

Details

The known universal lower bound 2/5 is explicitly transferred to the lower limiting graph-ball density of every OLD set at every center. A periodic construction selecting 12 of 30 vertices attains equality. The lower bound does not assume periodicity.
